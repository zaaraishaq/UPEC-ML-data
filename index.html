Bioinformatics & Machine Learning Framework

Step 1

Data Assembly

1,186 genomes fetched; 2 duplicate reference strains removed leaving 1,184 clean UPEC assemblies.

Step 2

Feature Matrix

73,218 binary features predicted (genes, pathways) using Prokka and Roary.

Step 3

FDR-Correction

Cohort restricted to STs represented by >10 isolates (838 genomes). Chi-Square test with FDR-correction (Benjamini-Hochberg) identified 28,230 significant features.

Step 4

Model & SHAP

Classifiers trained with 5-fold Stratified CV. SHAP model deployed to identify lineage markers.

Key Synergy Clusters

Hemolysin & CNF1 Island r = 0.94

Alpha-hemolysin core operon (hlyA, hlyC, hlyD) is highly co-occurrent with cytotoxic necrotizing factor 1 (cnf1) and fimbrial adhesins (sfa, pap). These co-occur on the pathogenicity island (PAI) IICFT073, causing massive tissue exfoliation.

CTX-M-15 Resistance Cassette r = 0.88

Extended-spectrum beta-lactamase blaCTX-M-15 displays strong co-occurrence with the insertion element ISEcp1, which drives its transposition, alongside aminoglycoside (aac(6')-Ib-cr) and fluoroquinolone resistance alleles.

Yersiniabactin Siderophore Operon r = 0.92

High-affinity iron chelating operon elements irp1, irp2, and its cognate outer membrane receptor fyuA form an absolute functional block. Yersiniabactin is critical for iron scavenging from host proteins in urine.

Antagonism & Exclusion Dynamics

An inverse relationship exists between extreme virulence profiles (e.g., K1 Capsule/Colibactin presence in ST95) and extended beta-lactamase carriage. High-risk multidrug resistant clones typically exhibit evolutionary tradeoffs, acquiring massive resistance plasmids while shedding larger virulence-heavy islands.

The Open Pangenome of UPEC

Proportional classification of 73,218 total predicted gene families

Core Genome (1,473 genes)

Conserved across ≥99% of isolates. Governs essential metabolic, translation, and replication functions.

Soft Core (660 genes)

Conserved in 95-99% of genomes. Often includes central fitness operons and structural cell-wall features.

Shell Genome (4,488 genes)

Present in 15-95% of genomes. Comprises lineage-specific fimbriae, flagella, and defense mechanisms.

Cloud Genome (65,969 genes)

Extremely rare (<15% isolates). Encompasses phage segments, insertion elements, and highly specific accessory genes.

In Submission

Prioritizing lineage-specific genomic features in uropathogenic Escherichia coli using comparative genomics and interpretable machine learning framework

Zaara Ishaq1, Samavi Nasir1, Memoona Abdullah1, Rameesha Khursheed1, and Amjad Ali1,*
1Integrative Biology Laboratory (IBG), Department of Microbiology and Biotechnology (MBT), Atta-Ur-Rahman School of Applied Biosciences (ASAB), National University of Sciences and Technology (NUST), Islamabad, Pakistan.

Study Synopsis & Objectives

Multi-Locus Sequence Typing (MLST) categories clonal lineages in UPEC, yet the specific accessory genes driving evolutionary fitness and clinical success remain partially understood. We address this by using machine learning models trained on 28,230 FDR-corrected genomic features of 1,184 global isolates. Interpreting the highest-performing Gradient Boosting classifier via SHAP values, we identify specific, lineage-specific genomic markers (such as adhesin alleles, unique iron-acquisition operons, resistance cassettes, and phage networks). This database hosts this rich metadata alongside models to facilitate rapid, web-based evolutionary tracking of MDR uropathogens.
